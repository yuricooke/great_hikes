## TL;DR

1. **Outdooractive is a B2B tourism software company with a consumer app attached.** Tourism
   boards, alpine clubs and parks fill it with "official" routes; that is its moat in the Alps.
   It is weak in the US and almost invisible in US search for famous hikes. Learn from its
   attribution and page structure; partner (affiliate, maybe Pro+ embeds) rather than license.
2. **US search for famous hikes is split three ways:** blogs (Earth Trekkers, HikingGuy, The
   Planet D, Much Better Adventures), tour operators/marketplaces (Bookatrekking, Intrepid,
   Follow Alice), and official sites (NPS, DOC NZ) for permit queries. AllTrails dominates
   "[trail] hike" queries in Google (5.2M organic visits/month per Semrush) but showed up little in
   our tool for planning queries.
3. **Where we can win:** specific planning questions (permits, bookings, parking, "when does it
   open"), freshness ("checked on" dates), multi-option comparisons, and credited photos.
   Head terms like "best hikes in the world" are taken by high-authority sites.
4. **Instagram:** big feature hubs (Beautiful Destinations) monetize as content agencies for
   tourism boards; none gives the photographer a permanent page with trip facts. That gap is ours.
5. **Best near-term partners:** Bookatrekking, GetYourGuide/Viator (tours, 8%+), AllTrails
   affiliate (Impact), Outdooractive affiliate (Awin, EU), AvantLink gear. License deals can wait.

## 1. Outdooractive deep dive

### 1.1 Product
- **Web + app** (iOS/Android) for 30+ activities; route finder, route planner, map, "Travel
  Guide" (region/POI pages), community. App store copy pitches "officially approved routes" and
  "official topo maps in 25 countries", plus BuddyBeacon live location sharing
  ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/outdooractive-hiking-trails/id1364846172)).
- **Scale:** company claims 16M+ community members ([corporate](https://corporate.outdooractive.com/en/?p=672));
  "more than 20 million" in later communications **(unverified, single source)**. Grew by buying
  ViewRanger (UK, 4.6M users), mountNpass (FR) and Sentres (South Tyrol)
  ([Cambridge Network](https://www.cambridgenetwork.co.uk/news/boost-uk-tourism-viewranger-and-outdooractive-join-forces),
  [OA blog](https://corporate.outdooractive.com/oa-blog-en/growth-opportunities-found-in-south-tyrol-outdooractive-acquires-tour-portal-sentres)).
- **Company:** Alpstein Tourismus GmbH & Co. KG, Immenstadt (DE), founder/CEO Hartmut Wimmer;
  150+ staff ([gust](https://gust.com/companies/outdooractive), [company](https://corporate.outdooractive.com/en/company)).

### 1.2 Content model: three sources, one label per item
| Source | Who | How it gets in | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Official / verified partners** | Tourism boards (DMOs), alpine clubs (DAV sections), parks, regions | They pay for a B2B account and publish into the shared platform via the "MyBusiness" backend; content flows to OA web/app, partner portals (alpenvereinaktiv, Bergfex) and their own sites ([module glossary](https://business.outdooractive.com/module-glossary)) | TMB stage hike by **DAV Sektion Dresden**, "Verified partner", "Responsible for this content" ([route](https://www.outdooractive.com/en/route/long-distance-hiking/montblanc-gruppe/stage-hike-tour-du-mont-blanc/43000451/)) |
| **Community** | Any registered user | Plans/records and publishes a route; others rate/comment | Tre Cime loop by community author Andrew Locking, 4.2/5 from 4 ratings ([route](https://www.outdooractive.com/en/route/hiking-trail/dolomiti-d-ampezzo-dolomiti-bellunesi/tre-cime-di-lavaredo/164691318/)) |
| **Editorial** | "Outdooractive Editors" (in-house), plus "Outdooractive Experts" (selected partners and community members, explicitly *not* paid ambassadors) | Researched stories, collections, "10 most beautiful hikes in X" lists | [Outdooractive Editors source page](https://julian-alps.com/en/source/outdooractive-editors/1010/), [Experts](https://corporate.outdooractive.com/en/outdooractive-experts) |

Key idea: **every item names a responsible source with a logo**, and the official/verified badge is
what users trust. The CEO describes the model as "Outdooractive provides the technology and
reach, DMOs fill it with content and look after guests"
([GSTC slides](https://www.gstc.org/wp-content/uploads/S8-Hartmut-Wimmer.pptx.pdf)).

### 1.4 Anatomy of a route page
Real example: **"Stage hike Tour du Mont Blanc"**
([outdooractive.com/…/43000451](https://www.outdooractive.com/en/route/long-distance-hiking/montblanc-gruppe/stage-hike-tour-du-mont-blanc/43000451/)), checked 2026-10-08:

1. Breadcrumb (Routes › France › Mont Blanc Massif), title, activity type, "Multi-day route".
2. **Action bar:** share, bookmark, flyover video, print, GPX/KML/FIT export, open in planner,
   similar routes, "joint trip", **embed**, report concern, donate.
3. **Responsible source** with logo and "Verified partner" badge.
4. **Key facts:** distance 114.4 km, duration 55 h, ascent 8,490 m, descent 8,670 m,
   highest/lowest point, difficulty (here SAC "T3 moderate"; other pages add rating bars for
   stamina, technique, experience, landscape).
5. Map + **elevation profile**; stage selector (11 stages, each a card with its own facts).
6. Tabs: *The route · Details · Roadbook · How to get there · Literature · Current information · Stages*.
7. Description, waypoints (huts, villages), **safety notes** (hut booking needed).
8. **Getting there:** public transport, directions, parking, coordinates (DD, DMS, UTM, what3words).
9. **Best season** month calendar.
10. **Literature & maps:** recommended guidebook (ISBN) and paper map (commerce hooks).
11. **User questions** (Q&A), reviews/ratings, "weather at the starting point" (10-day forecast),
    statistics.
12. "The 10 most beautiful hikes in [region]" and similar routes (internal linking).
13. **Photo credits on every image** (e.g. "Egon Höller, DAV Sektion Dresden"); note "Parts of
    this content were machine translated from German".

**Take-away:** it is the most complete fact sheet in the market, but dense, utilitarian and often
machine-translated. Photos are illustrative, not the point.

### 1.5 SEO strength
- Traffic is European: ~4M visits/month (Oct), **50% Germany**, then Italy 11%, Switzerland 9%,
  Austria 4%, France 4% ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/outdooractive.com/overview/)).
- In our US-index searches for "Tour du Mont Blanc itinerary/hike", "Tre Cime di Lavaredo hike"
  and German "Rundwanderung Drei Zinnen", **Outdooractive did not appear in the top results**;
  its pages only surfaced with a domain filter. Even in German, blogs, via-ferrata.de and the
  official South Tyrol site led. (Google.de likely ranks OA higher; **unverified**.)
- Its SEO weakness: thousands of near-duplicate community routes per famous hike (we found 10+
  TMB pages and 10+ Tre Cime pages), long URLs with numeric IDs, machine translation. Strength:
  sheer index size and region "best hikes" lists.

### 1.6 Business model
Software + geodata platform: **licence fees and services from 3,500+ B2B customers** (DMOs,
regions, clubs, publishers) in 25+ countries, plus B2C Pro/Pro+ subscriptions, premium content,
and ads in the free tier ([gust](https://gust.com/companies/outdooractive)). B2B modules: content
management, white-label websites, FlexView widgets, data API, analytics/heatmaps of visitor
activity, challenges, lead generation via Pro+ vouchers
([module glossary](https://business.outdooractive.com/module-glossary)). Revenue figures online
conflict and are not reliable **(unverified)**.

### 1.7 API / FlexView licensing
- **FlexView**: JavaScript widget (map, gallery, list) for 13 content types; **Data API** for raw
  export ([developers](https://developers.outdooractive.com/API-Reference/FlexView-API.html)).
- **Paid API licence required**; price not public. Rules: Outdooractive logo + "about" credit;
  source logo (≥50 px) and author on every detail page; photographer + licence on every image;
  **third-party content must be `noindex`**; daily sync
  ([Guidelines](https://developers.outdooractive.com/Overview/Guidelines.html)).
- Implication: licensed content gives us **no SEO value**. A Pro+ account (€60/yr) can embed
  routes on "your own website or blog", a much cheaper way to test an embed on a European page
  **(check the Pro+ terms allow commercial sites)**.

### 1.8 Audience: EU vs US
Core is DACH + Alps; US coverage thin (reviewers say it is "focusing on European content but
spreading into North America" and miss many trails in British Columbia —
[Pack and Trail](https://packandtrail.com/blog/outdooractive-gps-app-review/)). No US national
park or state tourism clients found **(unverified)**.

### 1.9 Strengths / weaknesses
| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Official, accountable sources; verified badge | Dense, dated UI; feels like a database |
| Most complete route fact sheet (transport, parking, season, literature, Q&A) | Duplicate community routes, machine translation |
| B2B lock-in with 3,500+ tourism customers | Weak in the US and in English-language search |
| Strict, visible attribution for text and photos | Photography is secondary; no inspiration layer |
| Embeds, GPX, flyovers, offline maps | Aggressive paywall complaints ([marlvel](https://marlvel.ai/apps/com-outdooractive-outdooractive)) |

### 1.10 Learn / copy / avoid
- **Copy:** "Responsible for this content" source block; photo credit on every image; the field
  set (getting there, parking, best-season calendar, current information, Q&A); stage cards for
  multi-day hikes; "10 most beautiful hikes in [region]" internal-link lists.
- **Learn:** official sources create trust; a "verified partner" label is something tourism
  boards will pay or contribute for later.
- **Avoid:** one page per user track (duplicates), machine-translated text, numeric-ID URLs,
  paywalling basics, a cluttered action bar.

### 1.11 Partnership possibility
Realistic now: **Awin affiliate** for Pro/Pro+ (5–50% of first-year net, 30-day cookie; DE/FR/AT/CH
programmes — [Awin](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/17147), [affilitizer](https://www.affilitizer.com/programs/outdooractive.com))
as the "navigate offline" button on European hike pages. Later (spec 012): a Pro+ embed or a
FlexView quote for Alps pages; long shot: become a content *source* on their platform (our
credited photos/trips under our logo) for reach in DACH.

### Who dominates
- **Tour operators/marketplaces** own multi-day international treks (Bookatrekking, Intrepid,
  Follow Alice, World Expeditions). Their guides are sales funnels, which is why their facts
  favour "book with a guide".
- **Blogs** own "best hikes in X" and single famous day hikes (Earth Trekkers, HikingGuy, The
  Planet D, Much Better Adventures, 10adventures).
- **Official sites + local news** own permit/lottery/booking queries (NPS, DOC NZ, KSL, KPCW).
- **AllTrails** barely appeared in our tool, but per Semrush it gets ~5.2M organic visits/month from
  2.4M keywords and 50% of its traffic is Google organic
  ([Semrush](https://cw.semrush.com/website/alltrails.com/overview)). Assume it owns "[trail name]
  trail" / "[trail] hike" in Google for US day hikes **(re-check in Google)**.
- **Outdooractive, Komoot, Wikiloc:** absent for English planning queries.

### Where a newcomer can realistically win
1. **Specific planning questions with dates:** "when do [X] permits open 2027", "Tre Cime parking
   reservation", "Milford Track booking date", "Angels Landing winter lottery". News sites win
   these with stale articles; a fact page with "checked on" date and the official link can beat them.
2. **Comparison/decision pages** operators avoid: "W vs O trek", "Machame vs Lemosho",
   "TMB self-guided vs guided", "Inca Trail alternatives without permits".
3. **Neutral facts on operator-dominated queries** (Inca, Kilimanjaro, Annapurna): operators
   contradict each other (e.g. TIMS card status), so a sourced, dated answer stands out.
4. **Visual long-tail:** "[hike] photos", "[hike] in October", "is [hike] worth it" — credited
   community photos per month/season are content nobody else has.
5. **Avoid for now:** "best hikes in the world", "best hikes in Banff/Yosemite" head terms (high
   authority needed); use them as hub pages for internal linking, not as traffic bets.

Generic "@hiking…"-style hub accounts exist in large numbers but none could be verified by search;
check sizes directly on Instagram before naming them in B3.

**Pattern:** hubs grow on reposted photos, credit only in the caption, and earn from **destination
and brand sponsorships**, not from their own website. Photographers get a tag and nothing durable.

**What @great_hikes should do to stand out**
1. **Every feature = a permanent page** (photo + credit + trip facts + map) and a "Featured on
   Great Hikes" story card the photographer reposts. No hub does this.
2. **Facts in the caption:** distance, gain, best months, permit note, "full guide: link in bio".
   Saves and shares beat likes for reach.
3. **Series formats:** "Permit Watch" (dates opening this month), "Hike of the Week", "This month
   in the Alps/Patagonia". Ties directly to SEO pages (§3).
4. **Credit rules as brand:** consent first, photographer handle in the first line, no
   uncredited reposts. Publicly state it in the bio; it differentiates from hubs that repost loosely.
5. **Monetize the Beautiful Destinations way, small:** sponsored collections for tourism boards
   or operators once at 10k, always labelled (B1 red lines).

## 6. Implications (mapped to specs and year-one goals)

1. **B3 · Permit & booking calendar as the SEO wedge.** Build ~20 "when/how to get permits for X"
   pages (Inca, Half Dome, Angels Landing, Narrows, Milford, Tre Cime parking, W Trek refugios)
   with official links and "checked on" dates; repurpose monthly as "Permit Watch" on Instagram.
   *Goals: traffic (O-1), 50 guides, IG 10k.*
2. **B4 · Adopt Outdooractive's fact sheet, without the clutter.** Trail/hike page order: hero
   photo with credit → key facts row → map + elevation → best-months calendar → getting there /
   parking → permits (official link) → current info → tips → Q&A/reviews → similar hikes. Add a
   visible "Source / responsible" block. *Goal: Core Web Vitals "good", trust.*
3. **008b · Stage cards for multi-day hikes, one canonical page per trail.** TMB, W Trek, AV1,
   Annapurna need stages; never one page per user track (avoid OA's duplicates). Add GPX
   download from OSM data (ODbL credit) and "Navigate in AllTrails / Komoot / Outdooractive"
